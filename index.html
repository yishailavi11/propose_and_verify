Abstract

Detecting multiple reflection symmetries in 3D shapes requires aggregating geometric evidence over a large space of candidate planes, making traditional voting approaches computationally expensive. We introduce the Fourier Accelerated Symmetry Transform (FAST), a training-free framework that combines geometric pair voting with efficient spectral computation. Exploiting the fact that global reflection planes pass through the shape centroid, we reduce the search to plane orientations, while geometric consistency filtering and independent witnesses suppress unreliable pair hypotheses. Our key insight is to represent these votes as a signal over plane orientations and efficiently compute a continuous, smoothed symmetry field in the spherical-harmonic domain. Specifically, we exploit the Funk-Hecke theorem, the spherical counterpart of the Fourier convolution theorem for rotationally symmetric kernels, to transform Gaussian smoothing into multiplication of harmonic coefficients. This enables efficient field evaluation, from which symmetry candidates are extracted, refined, and geometrically verified. Across two benchmarks, including a new benchmark of highly symmetric shapes, FAST achieves the highest clean multi-plane detection F1 scores among evaluated methods (0.861 and 0.947), with GPU inference below 4 ms per shape: over 100× faster than the next-fastest evaluated multi-plane GPU baseline. FAST also demonstrates strong robustness to geometric noise and competitive single-plane detection, achieving 94.9% success on clean single-plane shapes.

Method

  1. 01

    Screen & weight

    Surface-point pairs induce reflection planes. A centroid gate and independent witnesses weight geometrically consistent hypotheses.

  2. 02

    Vote over orientations

    Accumulate angular evidence on the projective sphere, where opposite normals describe the same plane.

  3. 03

    Filter harmonically

    Project the votes into spherical harmonics. Apply Gaussian smoothing through degree-wise multiplication.

  4. 04

    Refine & verify

    Refine local maxima continuously, then test reflected points against the input surface to retain reliable planes.
